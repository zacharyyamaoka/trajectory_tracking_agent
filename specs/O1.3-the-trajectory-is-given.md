# O1.3 The trajectory is given

## Requirement

> **A solution cannot retime, reshape or skip the trajectory** (layer 3 of the arm_motion_bench v2.0 layers: the
> trajectory and the robot are given; the levers are the torque and the controller).

From [OBJECTIVES.md §1 The black box](../OBJECTIVES.md#1-the-black-box), ruler v1. OBJECTIVES.md is the frozen ruler and the one source of every number; this note explains it and never overrides it.

## What it means

The tape is the target. A solution may look ahead along it (feed-forward, a lookahead), but the score compares the
measured tool point with the tape's own target at the tape's own instants ([O4.1](O4.1-objective-tool-track-rms.md)).
Slowing down, cutting a corner or skipping a via point all show up as tracking error, and the speed is fixed at 1.0
([O3.1](O3.1-dataset-workspace-v2.md)).

## Why

It keeps two problems apart: making a trajectory (planning and timing it) and following one. This agent works only on
the second; in OBJECTIVES.md's words, "the trajectory and the robot are given; the levers are the torque and the
controller". If a solution could retime the tape, a slower trajectory would read as better tracking.

## How it is checked

**No test proves it yet** (`tests: []`, so its status is *unverified*).

What holds it in code: `evaluate.py` scores against the dataset's own `task.trajectory`, on the tape's own sample
instants; the rung's input script hands the controller the trajectory once, at t = 0, and nothing after ("a solution
cannot be replanned", `_TaskScript`); and the tape's arrays are read-only numpy arrays, so an edit in place raises
(checked by hand on 2026-11-24, not by a test). No test runs a solution that retimes or edits the trajectory it was
given and checks it is scored against the untouched tape. Writing that test is in [ideas.md](../ideas.md) under
*I'll try*.
